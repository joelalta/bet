Compressed Air Supply System

Onboard Pneumatic Supply

The Compressed Air Supply System generates and manages the pneumatic pressure required by the BET and connected locomotive systems.

It allows electrically driven compressor equipment within the BET to support systems that rely on a stable supply of compressed air.

System Role

The compressors draw in atmospheric air, compress it and deliver it into the train's pneumatic network.

Stored air can then be made available when required without the compressors needing to run continuously. The system starts and stops automatically to maintain pressure within an approved operating range.

Operating Principle

When system pressure falls below its lower control threshold, the control system starts one or both compressors.

Air is compressed, conditioned as required and delivered to the pneumatic network or storage receivers. Once the upper pressure threshold is reached, compressor output can be reduced or stopped.

This automatic operating cycle maintains available pressure while limiting unnecessary compressor runtime and auxiliary energy consumption.

Battery System

Five Racks · 30 Battery Packs · Approximately 2.3 MWh

The BET Battery System stores the electrical energy used to power locomotive traction and onboard auxiliary systems. Its modular LFP battery architecture combines high energy capacity with integrated monitoring, thermal management and protective controls.

System Overview

Thirty battery packs are distributed across five structural racks within the BET. This modular arrangement supports practical installation, system monitoring and service access while packaging approximately 2.3 MWh of energy within the tender platform.

The battery system can receive energy through rooftop charging, plug-in charging and regenerative braking. During operation, stored DC energy is supplied to the vehicle's power-conversion equipment according to traction demand and the condition of the wider electrical system.

Each rack contains a repeated arrangement of battery packs, creating a scalable mechanical and electrical architecture. The five racks operate together as a coordinated energy-storage system rather than as isolated batteries.

Operational Role

During regenerative braking, the energy flow reverses. Electrical energy recovered by the traction system is returned to the BET and stored for later use, reducing the amount of braking energy otherwise dissipated as heat.

Integrated Cooling and Temperature Control

The Thermal Management System maintains suitable operating temperatures across the BET's principal electrical and energy-storage equipment.

Battery performance, charging capability and equipment life are all affected by temperature. The Thermal Management System helps maintain consistent conditions across the battery installation and power-conversion equipment.

This supports predictable performance and reduces temperature differences between components operating under different loads.

Where liquid cooling is used, coolant absorbs heat from connected equipment and transports it to the cooling assemblies, where that heat can be transferred to the surrounding air.
